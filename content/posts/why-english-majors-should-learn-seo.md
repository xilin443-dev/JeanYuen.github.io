When I first worked as an overseas headhunter, I encountered a problem I had never expected: I knew how to search, but I did not know what I was looking for.

At the time, I was working on recruitment related to the solar and renewable energy industry. The problem was that I was not familiar with the industry at all. I needed to identify outstanding companies in the field, but I did not even know which companies were worth looking at or where to begin my research.

So I did what many people would probably do today:

I asked AI.

AI quickly gave me a list of companies that seemed relevant to my search. At first, I was simply impressed by how quickly it could help me navigate an unfamiliar industry.

But then another question came to me:

Why did AI recommend these companies?

Why were these companies so visible when I had never heard of many of them before?

Did these companies simply happen to have a strong online presence? Were they investing in advertising? Or had they done something to make their information easier for search systems to find?

That was when I first became curious about SEO.

And as an English major, I began to wonder about something else:

Why should English majors learn SEO, and how could it actually help their careers?

What Is SEO—and Why Does It Matter in the Age of AI?

SEO, or Search Engine Optimization, is often associated with keywords and Google rankings. But it is much broader than simply putting keywords into an article.

At its core, SEO is about making information easier for search systems—and ultimately for people—to find, understand, and trust.

For example, when someone searches for “best solar companies in Europe,” thousands of companies may exist, but only some of them will appear prominently in the search results.

This raises an important question:

What makes some information more discoverable than other information?

SEO attempts to address exactly this problem.

It involves understanding what people search for, why they search for it, how information should be organized, and how websites can provide useful and credible answers.

And the rise of AI has made this question even more interesting.

When I asked AI to help me identify companies in an unfamiliar industry, I was not simply looking at a traditional list of search results. I was interacting with an AI system that interpreted my request and generated recommendations.

The exact mechanisms behind AI-generated recommendations vary from system to system. However, my experience made me realize that online visibility does not happen by accident. The way information is written, organized, published, and connected can influence how easily it can be discovered by both people and digital systems.

That realization led me to SEO.

Why Should English Majors Learn SEO?

At first, SEO may seem unrelated to an English degree.

English majors study literature, linguistics, translation, writing, and communication. SEO, on the other hand, sounds like something for marketers, programmers, or data analysts.

But there is actually a strong connection between the two.

1. English Majors Already Have One of SEO’s Most Important Skills: Language

SEO may involve data and technology, but the final product is often still language.

A search engine can help identify what people are searching for. But someone still needs to turn that information into content that is clear, useful, natural, and engaging.

This is where English majors have an advantage.

English majors spend years learning how to:

* write clearly and effectively;
* understand different types of texts;
* analyze meaning and context;
* adapt language to different audiences;
* communicate ideas across cultural boundaries.

These skills are highly relevant to SEO content creation.

Good SEO writing is not simply about inserting the right keywords. It requires understanding a much more fundamental question:

What does the reader actually want to know?

For example, someone searching for “how to choose a solar panel supplier” has a different intention from someone searching for “solar panel supplier jobs.”

The words may be similar, but the information they need is completely different.

Understanding language means understanding these differences in context—and that is something English majors already practice.

2. SEO Turns English Writing Into a More Marketable Skill

One reason English majors may benefit from learning SEO is that it can connect language skills with the digital economy.

Traditionally, when people think about career paths for English majors, they may think of teaching, translation, interpreting, or publishing.

These remain important career options, but they are not the only ones.

Adding SEO skills can open connections to areas such as:

* SEO content writing;
* content marketing;
* digital marketing;
* international marketing;
* localization;
* international communication;
* content strategy.

In other words, SEO can turn a general language skill into a more specific professional skill.

Knowing English is valuable.

Knowing how to use English to reach the right audience is even more valuable.

3. SEO Helps English Majors Understand How Information Travels

This is perhaps the most interesting connection for me.

As an English major, I have always thought about language as a means of communication. But SEO made me think about another part of communication:

How does information actually reach an audience?

Imagine that a Chinese renewable energy company wants to enter an overseas market.

Simply translating its Chinese website into English may not be enough.

The company may also need to ask:

* What are potential customers searching for?
* What words do they use?
* What information do they care about?
* How do people in different markets describe the same product?
* How should the company’s message be presented to an international audience?
* How can its content become easier to discover?

This is where translation, cross-cultural communication, content creation, and SEO begin to overlap.

Translation helps transfer meaning from one language to another.

SEO helps make that information discoverable.

And international communication connects it with the people who actually need it.

For English majors interested in global communication, this combination can be particularly meaningful.

How Can SEO Skills Help English Majors Build Their Careers?

Learning SEO does not mean that every English major needs to become a technical SEO specialist.

Instead, SEO can be viewed as an additional skill that makes existing language abilities more versatile.

Consider the difference:

English Major Skills	With SEO Skills
English writing	SEO content writing
Translation	SEO localization
Research	Keyword and competitor research
Editing	Content optimization
Cross-cultural communication	International content marketing
Audience awareness	Search-intent analysis

This combination can lead to different career directions.

SEO Content Writer

SEO content writers create articles and web content designed to answer users’ questions while also being discoverable through search.

For English majors who enjoy writing, this can be a natural way to apply their language skills in a digital environment.

Content Marketing Specialist

Content marketing goes beyond individual articles. It involves using content to attract, inform, and engage potential customers.

An English major’s writing and communication skills can become useful here, while SEO provides a framework for understanding how audiences discover that content.

International Marketing Specialist

For companies expanding into international markets, language is only one part of the challenge.

Companies also need to understand how people in different markets search for information and what kinds of content they respond to.

This creates a potential intersection between English, cross-cultural communication, SEO, and international marketing.

SEO and Localization

Localization is another interesting path for English majors.

Translation asks:

How can I express this message accurately in another language?

Localization goes one step further:

How should this message be adapted for a particular audience and market?

SEO adds another question:

How are people in that market actually searching for this information?

Together, these skills can make language professionals more involved in the entire communication process rather than only the translation stage.

Do English Majors Need to Become SEO Experts?

Probably not.

If you are an English major reading this and thinking, “I don’t know how to code, so SEO probably isn’t for me,” I would not start there.

SEO can be learned in stages.

Level 1: SEO Literacy

Start by understanding:

* what SEO is;
* how search engines work;
* keywords;
* search intent;
* basic on-page SEO.

Level 2: SEO Content Skills

Then learn how to:

* conduct basic keyword research;
* understand search intent;
* write SEO-friendly content;
* optimize titles and headings;
* structure an article clearly;
* analyze competing content.

Level 3: Technical SEO

If you become more interested in the field, you can move into:

* website analytics;
* crawling and indexing;
* website performance;
* structured data;
* technical optimization.

For many English majors, Level 1 and Level 2 are already valuable starting points.

The goal is not necessarily to replace your English skills with technical skills.

It is to combine them.

What My Head-Hunting Experience Taught Me About SEO

Looking back, I did not discover SEO because someone told me that English majors should learn it.

I discovered it because I had a practical problem.

I was unfamiliar with an industry. I needed to find relevant companies. I asked AI for help. And then I became curious about why certain companies appeared in the answer.

That experience changed the way I think about online information.

Before, I mostly thought about finding information.

After that experience, I started thinking about why some information is easier to find in the first place.

And that is where SEO became interesting to me.

For English majors, I think this is the most important reason to learn SEO.

We do not need SEO simply because we need another technical skill on our resumes.

We need to understand how language works in a digital environment where information is increasingly discovered through search engines, AI systems, websites, and online platforms.

English is a tool for communication. SEO helps us understand how that communication becomes visible.

For an English major thinking about the future, that is a skill worth exploring.
